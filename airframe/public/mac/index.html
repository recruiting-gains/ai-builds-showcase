START SMALL. STAY IN CONTROL.

Your first five minutes.

  1. Open Airframe Mac. It starts with the camera and system control off.
  2. Start camera preview. Approve Camera when macOS asks. Check the lighting and keep one open hand visible.
  3. Set up Accessibility. In System Settings → Privacy & Security → Accessibility, enable the exact Airframe Mac app you installed.
  4. Try pointer-only control. Leave “Allow left clicks and dragging” unchecked. Select your display, click Start Mac control, and wait through the countdown with an open hand.
  5. Then try a harmless click. Pause, enable clicking and dragging, and start again. Practice on an empty test window—not purchases, delete buttons, or sensitive work.
YOUR EXIT ROUTES

You keep the controls.

Escape stops camera and control while Airframe is focused, and system-wide while Mac control is armed.

Your physical mouse or trackpad pauses system control. The menu-bar Stop command remains available.

Lost tracking, stale frames, sleep, or permission changes pause/disarm control. It does not silently resume.

Releasing a held click can activate the item underneath. Stop checks reduce risk; they cannot make gesture input error-free or guarantee behavior after an app/OS crash.

What this version does—and doesn’t.

Moves the system cursor and optionally sends left-button clicks and drags across ordinary apps on your selected display. It is not a general AI agent: no right-click gesture, scrolling gesture, typing, app scripting, screen reading, remote access, or login-screen control.

The native companion uses Apple Vision; the browser playground uses MediaPipe. They are independent experiences, not a hidden link from a public webpage into your computer. Physical-camera reliability and emergency-stop behavior still need a consenting hands-on trial on your Mac.
